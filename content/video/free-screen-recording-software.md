## Start with recording length

Loom's free plan restricts recordings to 5 minutes and stores up to 25 videos. Choose **OBS Studio** for unlimited recording without a watermark, **ShareX** on Windows when screenshots and annotation belong in the same workflow, and **Loom Free** when a hosted viewer link matters more than recording length. **Screencastify Free** fits short classroom recordings made in Chrome.

Tutorials and product demos usually need recording length, export quality, and webcam overlay control, which points to OBS. Quick bug reports and async meeting updates often need cloud sharing more than editing depth, which is where Loom still earns a place. Windows users who capture screenshots and clips all day should look at ShareX before adding another single-purpose recorder.

---

## Why Loom's free tier changes drove people to search for alternatives

Loom combined recording, cloud hosting, and a shareable viewer link in one workflow, while OBS Studio saved local files and required separate sharing. Its earlier free tier allowed unlimited recordings and storage with a 25-video cap.

In 2023, Loom (acquired by Atlassian) tightened the free tier to 25 videos total and 5-minute recording limits per video. For anyone using Loom for product demos, onboarding videos, or training content, this change immediately pushed recordings over the limit.

The alternatives below offer unlimited recording in different ways: OBS Studio with no cloud hosting, ShareX with local file output, and Clipchamp as a simple Windows-native option. For editing recorded footage after capture, see our [free video editing software guide](/video/free-video-editing-software/) for the best free editors that work with OBS output files.

## The best free screen recording software in 2026

### 1. OBS Studio: best free screen recorder for most users

OBS Studio is a free, open-source screen recording and live streaming application used by professional content creators, streamers, and video producers worldwide.

**What you can record for free:**
- Unlimited recording length: no time caps
- No watermarks on output
- Record screen, webcam, or both simultaneously
- Multiple scene configurations (switch between layouts mid-recording)
- Audio mixing: capture desktop audio, microphone, or both independently
- Output formats: MKV, MP4, MOV, FLV, and more
- Resolution up to 4K
- Available on Windows, macOS, and Linux
- Completely free: no premium tier, no subscription

**Where you handle it yourself:**
- No built-in cloud hosting or shareable link: recordings save locally, you host them yourself
- Steeper setup curve than Loom or Screencastify: requires initial configuration of scenes and sources
- No built-in annotation tools during recording
- No automatic transcription or AI-generated summaries

**Best fit:** Anyone who needs unlimited, watermark-free screen recording for tutorials, product demos, course content, or any video that exceeds 5 minutes. Also the right choice for users who want full control over output quality and format.

OBS Studio supports the recording setups used for professional production while remaining free. Its scenes and sources system can hold several configurations, such as screen only, webcam overlay, and presentation mode. The audio mixer controls desktop and microphone levels separately. That flexibility is unnecessary for a quick clip, but useful when a recording workflow becomes more involved.

[Download OBS Studio free →](https://obsproject.com)

### 2. ShareX: best free screen recorder for Windows power users

ShareX is a free, open-source screen capture and screen recording tool for Windows with annotation, workflow automation, and over 80 upload destinations.

**What you can capture for free:**
- Unlimited screen recording: no watermarks, no time limits
- Screenshot capture with instant annotation tools (arrows, text, blur, highlight)
- Scrolling capture for long web pages
- GIF recording
- Optical character recognition (OCR) on screenshots
- Automatic file upload to Imgur, Dropbox, Google Drive, and 80+ destinations
- Hotkey customization for every function
- Workflow automation: capture, annotate, and upload in one keystroke

**The practical limit:**
- Windows-only: no macOS or Linux support
- Interface is complex: the breadth of features makes it harder to learn than simpler tools
- No built-in cloud hosting with a shareable viewer link

**Best fit:** Windows power users who want screen recording combined with screenshot annotation, and anyone who captures screens frequently enough that workflow shortcuts pay off.

**Workflow tradeoff:** ShareX combines recording, annotation, OCR, and configurable uploads in one Windows application. It is excessive for basic recording but can replace separate capture and annotation tools for someone who needs those steps regularly.

[Download ShareX free →](https://getsharex.com)

### 3. Loom Free: best for quick shareable recordings under 5 minutes

Loom Free is a cloud-based screen recording tool with an automatic shareable link, built-in viewer, and asynchronous video messaging features.

**What you can share for free:**
- Recordings up to 5 minutes in length
- Up to 25 videos in total storage
- Automatic shareable link generated after recording
- Clean in-browser viewer with comment and reaction support
- Screen, camera, or screen-plus-camera recording modes
- Chrome extension and desktop app available
- Basic transcript (AI-generated, accuracy varies)

**The practical limit:**
- 5-minute recording limit: hard cap, cannot be extended on free
- 25 video storage limit: oldest videos must be deleted to make room
- No custom branding or viewer customization on free
- Download of recordings requires paid plan on some configurations
- Drawing tools during recording require paid

**Best fit:** Teams sending short async video messages: status updates, quick feedback on designs, brief product walkthroughs under 5 minutes. The shareable link workflow is the feature that justifies Loom despite its limits.

Loom's shareable link experience is better than OBS or ShareX for quick communication. A Loom link opens in a browser, plays immediately, and allows the recipient to comment and react without downloading anything. For internal team communication where videos are short and the recipient experience matters, Loom's 5-minute free tier covers the use case. For anything longer, OBS is the answer.

[Try Loom Free →](https://loom.com)

### 4. Screencastify: best for educators and Chrome users

Screencastify is a Chrome extension-based screen recorder designed primarily for educators, with a free tier that allows unlimited recordings with some restrictions.

**What Chrome records for free:**
- Unlimited recordings (limited to 30 minutes each on free)
- Screen, browser tab, or webcam recording
- Saves directly to Google Drive
- Basic annotation during recording (pen tool)
- Shareable Google Drive link after recording
- Works entirely in Chrome: no desktop app download required

**The tradeoff to know:**
- Watermark on free recordings
- Advanced editing features require paid
- No MP4 export on free: recordings stay in Google Drive format
- Only works in Chrome: no other browser support

**Best fit:** Teachers who record lessons or feedback in Chrome and distribute them through Google Classroom or Drive. The recording lands in the same system students already use.

Screencastify keeps the path short: install the Chrome extension, record, and send the Google Drive link. The watermark on free recordings is the main trade-off. It matters less for a classroom explanation than for a client-facing video.

[Try Screencastify Free →](https://screencastify.com)

Clipchamp is Microsoft's video creation tool, built into Windows 11, which includes a screen recorder alongside basic video editing features.

**What Windows gives you for free:**

**Where it stays basic:**

**Best fit:** Windows 11 users who want a quick, no-install screen recorder for occasional use and basic edits without downloading anything.

**Workflow tradeoff:** Clipchamp is included with Windows 11 and needs no separate recorder installation. Choose it for occasional capture when OBS's scenes, sources, and audio routing would add unnecessary setup.

## A basic OBS Studio setup

OBS Studio's interface looks complex on first launch because it is built for live streaming with multiple simultaneous sources. For basic screen recording, the setup is straightforward:

Open OBS. In the Sources panel at the bottom, click the + icon and select Display Capture (to record your whole screen) or Window Capture (to record a specific app). If you want your webcam, add a Video Capture Device source and resize it to sit in the corner of the canvas. In Settings → Output, set Recording Format to MP4 for maximum compatibility. Click Start Recording. When done, click Stop Recording: your file is in the path shown in Settings → Output.

That is the complete setup for 95% of recording use cases. The scenes, transitions, and advanced audio mixing are there when you need them, but are not required for basic use.

Paid screen recording tools such as Loom Business, Camtasia, and ScreenFlow add built-in editing, chapter markers, interactive elements, and team management. They make sense for client-ready course content or product demos when moving recordings into a separate editor takes too much time.
