## Team size now decides the free tier

**Clockify** and **Toggl Track** both allow five free users, with Clockify leaning toward shared projects and Toggl toward a quick personal timer. **TimeCamp** removes the user cap for teams with a flat project list, and **RescueTime Lite** records desktop activity in the background instead of relying on manual starts and stops. Paid plans become relevant for approvals, payroll links, client reports, and administrative controls.

Freelancers should choose based on whether they need clean personal timers or invoices from tracked work. Agencies should look harder at project, client, and reporting limits. Teams need to know whether every person can track time for free, because per-seat pricing is where paid time tracking gets expensive quickly.


## Timers are usually free; administration is not

Time-tracking plans often leave timers free and charge for approvals, billing rates, invoicing, or administration. [Harvest's free plan](https://www.getharvest.com/pricing) is limited to one seat and two projects, while [Toggl Track's free plan](https://toggl.com/track/pricing/) supports up to five users. A team should compare those caps before choosing a tool that later charges per seat.

Those charges matter less when a freelancer invoices elsewhere or a small team needs only time logs. All five options below are ongoing free plans rather than trials.


## Five time trackers and their free boundaries

### 1. Clockify: shared tracking for up to five users

Clockify, from Cake.com, gives up to five users unlimited tracking and projects, according to its [current free-plan documentation](https://clockify.me/help/administration/subscription-plans).

What you can track for free:
- Up to 5 users
- Unlimited projects, clients, and tasks
- Manual time entry and timer mode
- Weekly timesheet view
- Reporting (summary, detailed, and weekly reports)
- Team time tracking: see all team members' hours
- Browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Edge)
- Desktop apps (Windows, macOS, Linux) and mobile apps
- Integrations with Trello, Asana, Jira, GitHub, and others

Locked to paid plans:
- Billing rates and revenue tracking
- Invoicing from tracked time
- Scheduled reports
- Time off and attendance tracking
- GPS tracking for field teams

It fits freelancers and teams of up to five that need shared time logs across projects and invoice from a separate tool. Its pricing page also limits free report ranges to one month, so teams that need longer reporting windows or a sixth active user face an upgrade decision.

[Try Clockify free →](https://clockify.me)


### 2. Toggl Track: best for individuals who want the cleanest interface

Toggl Track centers its workflow on a one-click timer across web, desktop, mobile, and browser extensions. Its free plan covers individuals and teams of up to five people.

The free plan covers:
- Unlimited time entries
- Unlimited projects and clients
- One-click timer from the web, desktop, or browser extension
- Basic reporting (summary view)
- Pomodoro timer mode
- Calendar integrations (see tracked time alongside calendar events)
- 100+ app integrations via browser extension (automatic tracking suggestions)

Paid plans add:
- Billing rates and revenue reports
- Scheduled PDF reports
- Project profitability tracking

Its browser extension can detect the app or website in use and suggest a matching project, which suits solo freelancers and consultants who want a focused timer. Clockify is the better fit when team reporting and broader project controls matter.

[Try Toggl Track free →](https://toggl.com/track)


### 3. RescueTime Lite: best for automatic background time tracking

RescueTime Lite never asks you to start a timer. The free desktop tier records time spent in apps and websites on its own; RescueTime's iOS app requires a paid plan.

The Lite tier records and reports:
- Automatic time tracking (no manual entry required)
- Activity reports: apps and websites tracked by category
- Weekly email report of time spent
- Basic productivity score
- Reports cover the most recent two weeks

Kept for paid plans:
- Distraction blocking and Focus Sessions
- Goals and alerts
- Detailed category customization
- Report history beyond two weeks

It can show time split between focused work, email, and browsing, which makes it a personal time-awareness tool rather than a source of client billing records.

[Try RescueTime Lite free →](https://rescuetime.com)


### 4. TimeCamp: unlimited users and top-level projects

TimeCamp is the only tracker here with no user cap on its free plan. Its [current plan comparison](https://www.timecamp.com/pricing/) includes unlimited users, unlimited top-level projects, manual entry, automatic tracking, and weekly timesheets on Free.

On the Free plan:
- Unlimited users
- Unlimited top-level projects; tasks and subtasks require paid
- Manual and automatic time tracking (desktop app detects apps and URLs)
- Weekly timesheets and PDF report exports
- Integrations with Trello, Asana, Jira, and others

Free projects stop at one level, though. Tasks, invoicing and billable time, budgets and profitability, attendance, time off, approvals, SSO, and priority support all sit on paid plans. The free plan suits a team that needs many people logging time against a flat project list and invoices elsewhere.

[Try TimeCamp free →](https://timecamp.com)


### 5. Harvest: best free plan for solo freelancers with built-in invoicing

Harvest is the one tracker here that turns entries into invoices and takes client payments on the free plan, but the free tier stops at one seat and two active projects.

Free for one seat:
- 1 seat (solo use only)
- 2 active projects at a time
- Unlimited invoicing from tracked time
- Expense tracking
- Payment via Stripe and PayPal
- Reporting on time and budget per project

A third active project, a second seat, or advanced team reporting needs a paid plan.

Payment reminders and pay-from-invoice links are included, so a solo freelancer with no more than two active projects can go from tracked time to a paid invoice in one tool.

[Try Harvest free →](https://harvestapp.com)

## When free time tracking is enough and when to pay

Free time tracking covers all core use cases for solo freelancers and small teams: logging hours, generating time reports, tracking time by project and client, and creating basic invoices. The paid tiers become relevant when you need billing rates and profitability reports across a team (Clockify Standard, Toggl Starter), advanced budgeting and forecasting (Harvest's team features), or enterprise features like SSO and compliance exports.

Every paid plan here is priced per user, so the jump is smallest for a solo tracker and largest for a team that has been sharing a free workspace. Per-user rates, as each tracker's US pricing page showed them in September 2026:

| Tracker | Lowest paid tier | Price | New on that tier |
|---------|-----------------|-------|--------------|
| Clockify | Standard | $5.49 per seat a month billed yearly | Billable rates and invoicing from tracked time; the $3.99 Basic plan adds admin controls only |
| Toggl Track | Starter | $9 per user a month billed yearly, $12 monthly | Billable rates, revenue and team reports, project time estimates |
| RescueTime | Solo Focus | $7 a month billed yearly, $9 monthly | Focus Sessions with distraction blocking, goals and alerts; Solo+ at $12 adds timesheets |
| TimeCamp | Starter | $3.99 per user a month billed yearly, $5.49 monthly | Unlimited tasks, invoicing, attendance and time off, Excel exports |
| Harvest | Teams | $9 per seat a month billed yearly, $11 monthly | Unlimited seats and projects, team reports |

Clockify counts every active or invited person on the team page as a paid seat, owner included, so a five-person free workspace becomes five seats at once.

For a freelancer or small team, Clockify Free or Toggl Track Free can cover timers and basic reports. For the time-to-invoice step, Invoice Ninja in the [free invoicing software guide](/business/free-invoicing-software/) builds invoices from tracked time.

For teams running multiple concurrent projects, Clockify can sit alongside [free project management software](/business/free-project-management-software/) until its five-user cap becomes a problem. If those hours are tied to leads, clients, or deal stages, a [free CRM tool](/business/free-crm-software/) helps keep the customer side organized before the work turns into an invoice.


## Separate time capture from billing

Keeping the timer and the invoice in separate free tools scales further than an all-in-one free plan. **Harvest** combines both, but only for one seat and two active projects, while **Clockify** or **Toggl Track** plus a free invoicing app holds up to five people. At the sixth person, those two start charging per seat, and **TimeCamp**'s uncapped but flat project list becomes the free alternative.

Shift scheduling and leave sit in HR tools instead; Homebase, in the [free HR software guide](/business/free-hr-software/), combines scheduling and time tracking on one free plan.
