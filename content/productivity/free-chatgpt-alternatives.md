## Different tasks need different assistants

All of these services handle everyday chat, so the reason to use one instead of ChatGPT has to come from somewhere else: a long document handled in one pass, a research answer with sources, or an assistant inside apps you already use. Writing, coding, web search, file uploads, privacy, and app connections matter more here than model names.

Several free services cover work that would otherwise require ChatGPT Plus. **Claude Free** suits long documents, **Microsoft Copilot** adds web-grounded answers inside Microsoft apps, and **Google Gemini** connects with Google services. **Perplexity AI** centers its answers on cited web sources. **Grok** adds real-time search of posts on X. The [free AI writing tools guide](/productivity/free-ai-writing-tools/) compares drafting and editing features separately.

Plan limits and model access change often. Before building a workflow around any free assistant, check the current free-plan limits for message volume, web access, file upload support, coding help, image tools, and privacy controls.


## Free assistants differ most at tools and context

OpenAI's [Free Tier FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9275245-chatgpt-free-tier-faq) lists web search, file uploads, data analysis, image generation, voice, and access to existing GPTs. Everyday text chats are currently unlimited subject to abuse safeguards, while tools have separate limits. Model access varies by plan, app, and rollout, so check what your account offers before choosing an alternative for a specific model. An alternative is useful when one of those tool limits interrupts the job or another service fits the surrounding apps better.

No single tool is best for every use case. Pairing two free tools, one for research and one for drafting, costs nothing and covers more than either does alone.

People supplement ChatGPT when they want citations attached to search results, connections to Google or Microsoft services, a different document workflow, or access after a file, image, voice, or research limit has been reached.


## Five free ChatGPT alternatives

### 1. Claude free (Anthropic)

{{< rating 3.5 >}}

Claude is the one to try when a task comes with conditions and exceptions that have to stay consistent across a long document.

Claude's free access includes:
- Access to Claude on web, mobile, and desktop
- Long-context conversations for working with documents and drafts, subject to current free-plan limits
- File upload for document editing, summarising, and Q&A
- Strong instruction-following for tone, format, and length
- Web access on the free plan
- [Claude Design, Slides, and Docs](https://claude.com/pricing)

Usage limits, and what Pro and Max add:
- Limits reset on a rolling five-hour window, and how far a session stretches depends on conversation length, attachments, and model choice
- Pro ($20 a month, or $17 a month billed yearly) adds Claude Code, Research, more models, and higher usage
- Free accounts can create up to five projects; Pro removes that cap
- Max, from $100 a month, gives five or twenty times Pro's usage and priority access at busy times

Heavy document work can use the variable session allowance quickly. Anthropic documents the [five-project free limit](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177-how-can-i-create-and-manage-projects).

[Use Claude free →](https://claude.ai)


### 2. Microsoft Copilot

{{< rating 4 >}}

Microsoft offers Copilot on the web, in Edge, and through Microsoft apps without a separate subscription:
- Free web and app access for general questions, writing, brainstorming, summarising, and image creation
- Web-grounded answers for current information
- Integration with Edge and Microsoft surfaces
- Available on iOS, Android, and web

Subscriptions add deeper Microsoft 365 access:
- Microsoft 365 Personal ($9.99 a month) puts Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook, with higher usage than the free service
- Microsoft 365 Premium ($19.99 a month) raises usage again and includes a limited allowance for Copilot's research and analysis agents
- Free usage is subject to Microsoft's current credits, limits, and peak-time access rules

Copilot fits people who already use Edge, Windows, Outlook, or Office web apps. Web-grounded answers are available in the free service, while deeper document, spreadsheet, and inbox integration belongs to eligible paid Microsoft plans.

[Use Microsoft Copilot free →](https://copilot.microsoft.com)


### 3. Google Gemini

{{< rating 4 >}}

What Gemini offers a free account depends on the account and region, but it can include:
- General access to Gemini's fast model for everyday prompts
- Access to Google's current Gemini models at standard free limits
- Connections to Search, Gmail, Docs, Android, and other Google services depending on account and region
- Google Search integration for research-backed responses
- Image understanding and generation with current free-plan limits
- Access across Android (default assistant) and web

Google's paid plans raise limits and context:
- Free chats have a 32,000-token context window; Google AI Plus ($4.99 a month) raises it to 128,000, doubles usage, and adds video generation
- Google AI Pro ($19.99 a month) has a 1 million token window, four times the free usage, Gemini in Docs, and higher limits for the Jules coding agent
- Deep Think, which reasons in parallel and can take minutes per answer, is limited to Google AI Ultra, from $99.99 a month
- Limits on every plan refresh every five hours up to a weekly cap, and heavier models and features use more of them

Gemini fits accounts that already use Gmail, Docs, Drive, and Android. Connections and in-app features depend on the account, region, and subscription. Google's [Gemini limits page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805?hl=en) is the current reference for model, context, and feature access.

[Use Google Gemini free →](https://gemini.google.com)


### 4. Perplexity AI

{{< rating 4 >}}

Perplexity attaches source links to every answer by combining generated text with real-time web search.

Perplexity's free search service provides:
- Basic searches without a published monthly quota
- Source citations with every answer: links back to original pages
- Follow-up questions within the same search thread
- File upload for document Q&A (limited on free plan)
- Mobile apps for iOS and Android

Advanced searches and model choice are limited:
- The current plan comparison lists three free Pro Searches per day; file uploads are limited separately
- Pro ($17 a month billed yearly) adds deep research, a choice of models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and xAI, and document and app creation
- Max ($167 a month billed yearly) adds frontier models and the highest usage limits

Perplexity fits research that needs links back to source pages. Its citations make verification possible, but they do not guarantee that a generated summary represents the source correctly. Open the cited pages before using a claim, and check the [current plan comparison](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/11187416-which-perplexity-subscription-plan-is-right-for-you) and your account's allowance. The comparison lists three daily Pro Searches, while an older account guide still lists five. Our [free note-taking apps guide](/productivity/free-note-taking-apps/) covers places to organize the resulting notes.

[Use Perplexity AI free →](https://perplexity.ai)


### 5. Grok (xAI)

{{< rating 3.5 >}}

Grok's search covers posts on X as well as web pages, which helps with a story that is still developing and has little written about it yet. It runs at grok.com, in the iOS and Android apps, and inside X.

A free account gets:
- Real-time web and X search, at a lower allowance than paid plans
- Voice mode
- Image generation with Grok Imagine; every generated image carries a Grok watermark that cannot be removed
- File uploads in any chat, including PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, code, image, and audio files up to 150 MB each
- Connectors for Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Outlook, OneDrive, and Microsoft Teams, which xAI makes available to all Grok users

Usage and privacy boundaries:
- Premium at $30 a month lists video creation and higher usage
- xAI lists a shared weekly usage pool across Grok and Cursor for Chat, Imagine, Voice, and Bot; it does not publish the Free allowance or per-feature quotas
- xAI says consumer content may be used to train its models. Signed-in users can opt out for new conversations in Settings or use Private Chat, according to its [consumer FAQ](https://x.ai/legal/faq)

Reach for Grok when the question is about news or public reaction, or when you want an assistant that can search an inbox or cloud folder without a subscription. Posts on X are unverified, so a claim that rests on one needs a second source. Check the data controls under Settings before uploading anything confidential; xAI's [plan comparison](https://x.ai/pricing) shows the paid tiers and shared usage pool.

[Use Grok free →](https://grok.com)

## What paid plans add beyond higher limits

The main paid plans cost between $10 and $30 a month, and each unlocks something its free plan lacks entirely: a newer reasoning model, a coding tool, a longer context window, or video. The prices below are US list prices checked in September 2026. Several vary by country, and some drop with yearly billing.

| Service | Main paid plan | Adds over free | Next tier up |
|---------|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| ChatGPT | Plus, $20 a month | More reasoning and tool access, fuller Codex and deep research | Pro, $100, $200, or $500 a month |
| Claude | Pro, $20 a month | Claude Code, Research, more models and usage | Max, from $100 a month |
| Microsoft Copilot | Microsoft 365 Personal, $9.99 a month | Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook | Premium, $19.99 a month |
| Google Gemini | Google AI Pro, $19.99 a month | 1 million token context, video generation, Gemini in Docs, higher Jules limits | Ultra, from $99.99 a month |
| Perplexity | Pro, $17 a month billed yearly | Deep research, model choice, document and app creation | Max, $167 a month billed yearly |
| Grok | Premium, $30 a month | Latest models and higher limits | Super, $100 a month; Ultra, $200 a month |

ChatGPT Go, priced by country and possibly carrying ads, raises the free limits on uploads, images, and memory. Plus adds more reasoning and tool access. As of October 26, 2026, [Pro offers $100, $200, and $500 monthly tiers](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing), with different usage allowances. Check the current plan details for model access and limits rather than assuming one fixed multiplier applies to every tool.

Grok's [current pricing](https://x.ai/pricing), checked on October 26, 2026, also includes Plus at $8 a month below Premium. Super and Ultra raise usage further.

For code, Claude Code comes with every paid Claude plan and runs in the terminal on the same usage pool as chat. ChatGPT includes limited Codex access even on Free, with more from Plus, and Google AI Pro raises the limits on Jules, a coding agent Google still labels beta.

For visual work, Claude Free includes Design, Slides, and Docs within its free usage allowance. Grok makes images on its free plan and lists video creation on Premium. Gemini's video generation starts lower, at the $4.99 Google AI Plus plan.

The upgrade worth paying for is the one whose paid features you would use every week: Claude Pro for Claude Code or higher usage, ChatGPT Plus for more reasoning and tool access, Google AI Pro when the work sits in Docs and Drive, and Google AI Plus or Grok Premium for video. If the only problem is running out of messages, a second free service from this list covers the overflow, and the dedicated apps in the [free AI writing tools guide](/productivity/free-ai-writing-tools/) handle drafting and editing on their own free tiers. Check [OpenAI's current plan page](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/) before subscribing, because model access changes with each release.


## Research, drafting, and platform fit

A practical free setup is ChatGPT plus one alternative chosen for the limit you hit most often. Long documents point to **Claude**, answers you need to verify point to **Perplexity**, files that live in Google or Microsoft accounts point to **Gemini** or **Copilot**, and questions about what people are posting right now point to **Grok**.
